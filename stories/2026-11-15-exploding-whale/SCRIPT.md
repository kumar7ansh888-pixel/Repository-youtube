# They Blew Up a Whale… With Dynamite

Hook title: THEY BLEW UP|A WHALE?!

## Script
They blew up a dead whale… with dynamite.
A giant whale washed up on the beach… and it stank.
So the highway guys had a brilliant plan.
Blow it to bits… and let the seagulls eat the rest.
One man with army demolition training warned them. Bad idea.
They used half a ton anyway.
Oregon, 1970. Crowds lined the dunes to watch.
Then… BOOM.
The sand flew sky high… and then it rained whale.
Rotten blubber splattered everyone. People ran screaming.
And the seagulls? Scared away by the boom.
Most of the whale… was still lying there.
But wait, it gets worse.
One huge chunk flew over the dunes… and flattened a brand-new car.
Whose car? The guy who warned them.
He had just bought it at a sale called… a whale of a deal.
Years later, the town named a park after it… Exploding Whale Memorial Park.
Because nobody will ever forget the day…

## Facts (checked)
- 12 Nov 1970, Florence, Oregon: the Oregon Highway Division blew up a beached 45-ft, ~8-ton sperm whale with half a ton (20 cases) of dynamite,
  expecting scavenger birds to clean up the small pieces; the boom scared the birds off, blubber rained on spectators, reporters and cars, and most
  of the whale stayed on the beach (Wikipedia "Exploding whale"; KATU / OPB 50th-anniversary coverage).
- A WWII veteran with demolition training (Walter Umenhofer) warned the engineer and was brushed off; a ~300 lb chunk crushed the roof of his new
  1970 Oldsmobile, bought from a dealer advertising "a whale of a deal"; nobody was hurt and the state paid for the car (Jalopnik; Audacy Connecting Vets).
- 2020: Florence residents voted to name a new park "Exploding Whale Memorial Park" (Wikipedia; Eugene Weekly; Live Science).
- Not named on screen (private person): the car owner is "the guy who warned them".
