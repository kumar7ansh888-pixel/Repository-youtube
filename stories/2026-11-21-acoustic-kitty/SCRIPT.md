## Script
The CIA turned a cat… into a spy.
This is real. Declassified.
Step one: surgery.
A microphone… inside its ear.
A radio transmitter… at the base of its skull.
The antenna? Woven through its fur… down to the tail.
The mission: stroll up to enemy agents… and listen.
Who suspects a cat?
One problem. It's a cat.
It got bored. It got hungry. It wandered off.
So they operated again… to fix the hunger.
Then training. Five years of it.
Finally, the big test.

They open the door. The cat hops out…
Wait, it gets worse.
It never even reached the bench.
The total bill? Reportedly twenty million dollars.

But their big discovery? Cats can be trained… to walk short distances.

## Sources
- History.com (Becky Little), "The CIA's Plan to Turn a Cat Into a Spy…" (Operation Acoustic Kitty; mic in the ear, transmitter at the base of the skull, antenna woven into the fur; staffers drove it to a park to record two men on a bench; heavily redacted memo: "would not lend itself in a practical sense to our highly specialized needs")
- Smithsonian Magazine Smart News 2017 (3/4-inch transmitter at the base of the skull, mic in the ear canal, wire antenna woven through the fur to the tail; wandered off when bored, distracted or hungry; "hunger issues were addressed with another operation"; five years; total cost estimated at $20 million; 1967 memo "Views on Trained Cats": that cats "can indeed be trained to move short distances" was "in itself a remarkable scientific achievement"; first trip: never made it to the target)
- TIME 2017 (mid-1960s; Victor Marchetti: "would walk off the job when he got hungry"; agents in a van with dials; declassified by the National Security Archive in 2001)
- The ending (the disputed taxi story) is left off screen: the script only says the cat never reached the bench, which all three sources agree on.
