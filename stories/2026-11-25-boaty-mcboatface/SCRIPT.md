## Sources
- ITV News, "'Boaty McBoatface' tops poll as ship naming competition closes" (17 Apr 2016): 124,109 votes; Poppy-Mai 34,371; Henry Worsley 15,231; "It's bloody cold here" 10,679; David Attenborough 10,284; other entries "Usain Boat", "I Like Big Boats & I Cannot Lie", "What Iceberg?"; £200 million ship; suggested by former BBC radio presenter James Hand, who has since apologised.
- Global News / AP (Apr 2016): "won with 124,109 votes — more than three times its closest contender"; 200 million-pound ship; the vote was advisory; James Hand apologised.
- IBTimes UK (Apr 2016): 124,109 votes, 89,738 more than Poppy-Mai (34,371); £200m polar research ship; started as a joke suggestion; Hand apologised.
- NPR, "Boaty By Another Name" (6 May 2016): science minister Jo Johnson wanted a more "suitable" name; official name Royal Research Ship Sir David Attenborough; "Boaty McBoatface will live on as the name of one of the high-tech remotely operated sub-sea vehicles".
- NPR (18 Jun 2019): "the autonomous yellow submarine"; April 2017, Southern Ocean near Antarctica, depths up to 4,000 m; PNAS study found a previously unknown way stronger winds make deep water warm (sea-level rise).
- Smithsonian Magazine, "Boaty McBoatface Completes Its First Mission" (2017): Autosub Long Range, Orkney Passage, 7-week mission.
- CNBC, TIME, UPI (Jul–Oct 2017): Swedish rail operator MTR Express named a train "Trainy McTrainface" after a public vote, a nod to Boaty.
- Kept out: the presenter's name (living private person, not needed), the Sydney "Ferry McFerryface" (not verified from two sources), the $300m NPR figure (other sources say £200m).
- On-screen numbers only: 124,109 and 34,371 (ITV / IBTimes), £200M.
