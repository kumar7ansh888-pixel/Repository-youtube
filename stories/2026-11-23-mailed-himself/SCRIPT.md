## Script
This man mailed himself across the world.
No ticket. No seat. Just a wooden box.
He's a javelin champion, stuck in London… and totally broke.
A flight home? Way too expensive.
But his little daughter's birthday is coming.
So his buddy builds a crate… the biggest size planes allow.
Tiny problem? The box is shorter than he is.
He squeezes in with canned food, water… and empty bottles.
Why empty bottles? …Don't ask.
Day one: thick fog. The plane doesn't even take off.
He just sits there. In the box. For a whole extra day.
Wait, it gets worse.
Then Paris. Then Bombay… where workers leave him upside down.
In the blazing sun. For hours.
Sixty-three hours later… he lands in Perth.
He crawls out of the cargo shed… sneaks away… and hitchhikes home.
His buddy tells the newspapers… and he's a national hero.
Cheapest trip ever. Just ask…

## Sources
- Wikipedia, "Reg Spiers": Australian javelin thrower, went to England for the 1964 Olympic qualifying, penniless by end of season, wanted to be home for his daughter's birthday; crate built by friend John McSorley; nearly dehydrated on the runway in Mumbai; 63-hour stowaway flight; highly publicised after McSorley contacted James Coote of The Telegraph.
- Jo Usmar (Substack, 2023, from the BBC and the "Criminal" podcast): couldn't afford a ticket (~£350); injury; largest box allowed 5 ft x 3 ft x 2.5 ft, Reg taller; bottles of water and an empty one for urine, tins of baked beans; 24-hour fog delay in London; Paris, Bombay (upside down in the sun ~4 h), Singapore, Perth; crawled out of the bond shed, hitchhiked; folk hero.
- Michael W Travels 2015 (via USA Today Road Warrior Voices): 5'x3'x2'6" (max size), canned food + bottled water, used empty bottles and cans to pee into, 24-hour fog delay in London, London–Paris–Bombay, left upside down on the tarmac in the hot sun for hours, 63 hours, got out of a cargo shed and hitchhiked home, daughter's birthday.
- Vice/Broadly: injured javelin hopeful in London, friend helped build a wooden crate, daughter's birthday coming up.
- Kept out (only one source or disputed): "cash on delivery" / fake company label, Air India asking for the fare, exact food (beans vs spaghetti), his height (6 ft 1 vs over 2 m), his later life.
